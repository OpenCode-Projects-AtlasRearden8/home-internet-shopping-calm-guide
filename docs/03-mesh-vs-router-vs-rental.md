# 03 · Mesh vs Router vs Rental: Which Box Should You Have

*The plan you pay for is only the promise. The box is the delivery.*

If "the Wi-Fi is bad in the guest room" is your actual complaint, speed tiers
won't fix it. This guide turns box-deciding into a 10-minute, three-question
decision.

## Translation first

- **Modem:** the translator between your line (coax/fiber/satellite) and
  Ethernet. Fiber customers technically have an **ONT**; cable customers have
  a **cable modem**. Same job, different letters.
- **Router:** creates your home Wi-Fi network and shares the connection.
- **Gateway:** the provider's +modem+router welded together. The black box
  that rental fees love.
- **Mesh:** multiple Wi-Fi towers talking to each other, so one network's
  name covers your whole home. Great for overlap, slightly more expensive.
- **Extender:** a cheap repeater. It *gently* repeats signals and cuts speed
  where it repeats. Our honest rating: usually the wrong tool.

## The 10-minute decision

**Question 1 — Who must I serve?**
- One-person apartment/condo under ~1,500 sq ft: a **single good router**
  (or the provider's gateway) is realistically fine.
- House 1,500–3,000 sq ft with dead corners: **router + one mesh node**, or
  a small **2-pack mesh** if walls are brick, plaster, or drywall city.
- Homes over ~3,000 sq ft, or multi-floor with thick walls/E-shaped layouts,
  or outbuildings: **mesh with 3+ nodes**. Not optional. Experiencing
  "the speed is fine but the pizza looked frozen mid-frame" means coverage,
  not bandwidth.

**Question 2 — Fibre, cable, or wireless line?**
- **Fiber:** providers often include the ONT. You usually still want your own
  router (or to convert the gateway to bridge mode). Fiber's upload is
  wasted on a weak router.
- **Cable:** rental gateway is the easy button; a *separate* modem + your own
  router is the long-term money move if the provider lists compatible modems.
- **Fixed wireless / satellite / LEO:** coverage inside is usually fine with
  the included hardware; add mesh *later* only if tests show dead zones.

**Question 3 — Dishwasher test: what does your budget look like?**
- Rent ($5–$15/mo forever ≈ $60–$180/year): fine for flexibility and free
  support/replace. Choose this if you move within 12 months or never want to
  think about boxes.
- Own a plain mid-tier router ($80–$150, e.g., a current WiFi-6/6E model):
  pays for itself vs rental in roughly 1–2 years. Choose this for smaller
  homes and everyone comfortable plugging cables.
- Own a mesh system ($150–$500 for a decent 2–3 pack): choose this when
  Question 1 says "coverage problem" and you plan to stay put 2+ years.

## Rental vs own, side by side

| Factor | Rent the gateway | Own your router/mesh |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | $0 | $80–$500 |
| Ongoing | $5–$15/mo | $0 (electricity only) |
| Replacement if it dies | Provider's problem, usually free | Yours (but rare) |
| Support calls | They can see their hardware | Less hand-holding (they'll still test your line) |
| Performance | Often *good enough*, not great | Choice your speed deserves |
| Making it go away if moving | Just return it | Comes with you |

## If you keep the rented gateway (totally fine)

Two friendly upgrades:
1. Ask the provider to turn it into **bridge mode** — the gateway stops
   doing Wi-Fi and just feeds your better router. Removes double-NAT brains
   and fights; technically simpler than it sounds.
2. If bridge mode is a hassle, at least rename the gateway's Wi-Fi network
   to the *same* network name and password as your mesh/router, or see if
   the gateway's app lets you disable its Wi-Fi antennas. Two overlapping
   networks with different names = phones holding on to the weak one.

## If you buy: the 4 numbers that matter (ignore the rest)

1. **Wi-Fi 6 or newer** (6/6E/7). WiFi-6 sounds like a chore; it's a step.
2. **AX rating** tells you rough capacity — bigger number, more concurrent
   devices. Two-person homes: AX3000-class is plenty. Bigger families: higher.
3. **Ethernet ports**: want 2+. Check for 2.5G Ethernet if you have gigabit+
   fiber. Wired > wireless whenever a cable fits.
4. **Coverage rating:** honest review sites; marketing square-footage claims
   assume an open field. Halve them for real walls and real furniture.

Skip marketing checkboxes like "AI-powered" and game-fiers. The four numbers
above are the decision; $100 less than "the top model" is nearly always
invisible in a house.

## Wired beats wireless (the unbalance we never mention enough)

Wherever a cable fits, run one — TVs, consoles, desktops, streaming boxes,
the printer nobody fixes. One Ethernet line per thing makes wireless faster
for everything else by subtraction. If you own the walls (your house, not a
rental), this is the single biggest quality upgrade available.

## Placement, the cheap 25-minute fix

Before buying anything mesh-adjacent:
- Move the router: **center, elevated, not inside a cabinet, not next to the
  microwave** (2.4 GHz and microwaves are not friends).
- Antennas: one vertical, one horizontal — real physics, not decoration.
- Re-test your three speed tests (Guide 01). Sometimes this is the whole fix.
