# Start Here: A Calm Guide to Buying or Switching Home Internet

*By Asdale Dynamics, LLC — plain English, step by step, never condescending.*

Switching internet providers has a reputation for being stressful. It doesn't
have to be. Almost all of the stress comes from three things: not knowing how
much speed you actually need, getting confused by promo pricing, and dreading
the phone call to cancel. This guide covers all three, slowly, in order.

Nothing here requires technical skill. No jargon without translation. No
deadline pressure — every step can be done in 20-minute chunks.

## The calm path (the whole thing, in order)

1. **One speed test, three checks.** Test your current speed, then compare it
   to what you pay for (Guide 01). Most people discover they're either
   overpaying for speed they don't use, or underpaying for speed that makes
   everything buffer.
2. **Count your people and devices.** Two adults, three phones, a TV, a work
   laptop and a Xbox is a different household than one person with a tablet.
   Guide 01 has a table for your size.
3. **Decode the price.** For every plan you're considering, write down: price
   month 1–12, year 2 price, equipment fee, contract yes/no, and the "goodbye
   fee." Guide 02 gives you a decoder checklist.
4. **Decide the box situation.** Rent, buy a plain router, or buy a mesh
   system. Guide 03 makes this a 10-minute decision.
5. **Shop the boring way.** Fill in the [comparison sheet](06-printable-sheets.md)
   for 2–3 real plans. Seeing them on one printed page beats 40 open tabs.
6. **Call, politely.** Whether you're canceling your old provider or
   negotiating a better rate, Guide 05 has the words. You read the script;
   they read their script; you win because yours is calmer.
7. **Set a calendar reminder.** On switch day: return the old equipment,
   keep the receipt, and note the day the old autopay should stop. That's it.
   You're done, and next year you're not surprised by anything.

## Ground rules we promise to follow

- **No fear, no hype.** No "you MUST switch now." If your current setup works,
  this guide will tell you that too — the calm answer is sometimes "stay put."
- **Numbersd before names.** We talk in Mbps, price-per-year, and dollars
  saved, not about which company is "best" — offers change too fast.
- **You can quit for today.** Every guide ends with a stopping point. Save
  your sheet, close the tab, come back tomorrow. Nothing bad happens.

## Quick answers to the questions everyone asks

**"How fast does my internet need to be?"** Use the table in
[Guide 01](01-mbps-reality-check.md). Short version: 1–2 person households are
fine with ~100–200 Mbps; a family of four streaming and gaming usually wants
300–500 Mbps; you only "need" gigabit if you have many heavy users at once or
huge file uploads.

**"Is the intro price real?"** Yes, the intro price is real — but it expires.
[Guide 02](02-promo-pricing-and-contracts.md) shows how to compute the honest
12- and 24-month average and read the fee stack.

**"Do I have to call to cancel?"** Sometimes not (many providers let you
cancel online or by chat). But when you do call, the script in
[Guide 05](05-cancel-scripts.md) handles the retention conversation politely.

**"Is mesh Wi-Fi worth it?"** Sometimes. It depends on your home size and
layout, not on marketing. Guide 03 has a decision table.
