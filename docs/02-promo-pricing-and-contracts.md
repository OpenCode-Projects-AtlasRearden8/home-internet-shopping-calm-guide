# 02 · Promo Pricing & Contracts, Decoded

*"How much will this actually cost, twelve months from now?" is the only
pricing question that matters. Everything else is theater.*

Intro prices are real. So is the schedule hiding behind them. Here's how to
read it calmly.

## The anatomy of an internet bill

A plan's "price" is actually a stack. Write down every layer before deciding:

| Layer | What it is | Typical range (see provider for current values) |
|---|---|---|
| Intro price | The advertised number | Often valid 6–24 months |
| Standard price | What it becomes when the promo expires | Frequently $10–$40/mo higher |
| Equipment fee | Modem/router/gateway rental | ~$5–$15/mo (often $0 if you own) |
| Installation | Setup charge, waived with self-install | ~$0–$100 |
| Activation fee | Paperwork fee with a nicer name | ~$0–$100, sometimes waivable |
| Regional/broadcast sports surcharges | Shows up on some bundles | Varies; ask |
| Taxes | Unavoidable, modest | Varies by location |
| Early termination fee (ETF) | The contract's teeth | Often ~$10/mo remaining on contract |

## The two honest averages

When any plan is in view, compute **Avg12** and **Avg24**. This turns
theater into arithmetic:

- **Avg12** = (promo total you'll pay in months 1–12 + months 13–24 at
  standard price) ÷ 12 — wait, that's why we do it in two steps:
  - Avg12 = (12 × promo monthly + one-time fees) ÷ 12
  - Avg24 = ((12 × promo) + (12 × standard) + one-time fees) ÷ 24
- The Ads compare Avg12 to your old plan's Avg12. The **bill you actually
  live with** is closer to Avg24.

**Worked example (fictional numbers the way ads write them):**
"$49.99/mo for 12 months, then standard rate applies. $9.99/mo gateway fee.
$0 install with self-install."

- Months 1–12: 49.99 + 9.99 = $59.98
- Months 13–24: standard price (say) $79.99 + 9.99 = $89.98
- **Avg24 = ($59.98×12 + $89.98×12) ÷ 24 = $74.98/mo.**

That $49.99 ad is a $74.98 life. Still possibly a fine deal! You just deserve
to know it in the first ten seconds, not the thirteenth month.

## Contracts: what one actually means

Most major U.S. home internet plans today are **no-contract** — but packages
can still include them, usually as a trade for a bigger promo, a gift card,
or a phone bundle.

- **The ETF is the tell.** Early termination fees are commonly charged as
  ~$10–$15 per *remaining full month*, so $150 if you break out 12 months
  early. Ask for the current published schedule.
- **A no-contract plan can still have a promo trap.** No ETF, but the promo
  is a *term commitment in spirit*: some providers offer a discount that ends
  if you change plans — including upgrading speeds. That's a contract with
  friendlier paperwork.
- **Contracts that help you** (rare but real): fixed-some-rate-for-2-years
  deals can be genuinely good when the rate is ~Avg24 and the ETF math is
  humane. Evaluate the same way: compute Avg24 and look at month 25.

## The fee stack questions to always ask (script-ready)

Say or send exactly this to any provider's sales rep or chat:

> "For [PLAN NAME], can you confirm: (1) monthly price for each of the first
> 24 months, (2) equipment fee and whether I can use my own, (3) one-time
> fees (install, activation), (4) any surcharges or local fees specific to my
> address, (5) contract or ETF — yes/no and the number, and (6) whether any
> price is conditional on things like autopay or bundling?"

Bonus polite move: "If any answer applies only for new customers, tell me —
I may qualify as new under a different name or address." (Do not misrepresent
yourself, just ask how their rules work.)

## Autopay, bundles, and other small hinges

- **Autopay discounts are real and usually fine.** The catch: promo pricing
  sometimes *requires* it. Use a dedicated "bills" debit card if autopay
  makes you nervous.
- **Bundles** (TV + internet, phone) save money only if you'd buy the
  extras anyway. Standalone TV live-stream services or nothing usually wins
  in 2026. Always compare bundle Total24 vs standalone Total24.
- **Paper statements** sometimes add ~$2–$5. Go digital.
- **"Intro price ends when standard price rises"** — some standard rates
  themselves can rise again. Ask: "Is the standard rate in months 13–24
  fixed, or subject to change with notice?" Companies hate this question.
  Love it accordingly.

## The catch-and-release list (red flags)

- Price "for 12 months" but *nothing* to show what month 13 is → ask.
- "Free premium streaming for 12 months" → value it at what you'd actually
  watch and add to cost, not as a discount.
- "Free installation" that requires a technician who sells you "upgrades."
  If you have a working Ethernet/coax drop, self-install is usually fine.
- Gift cards as the *whole* pitch — they stretch Avg12 prettily but arrive
  months late and don't lower the bill.
- Any word like "commitment," "term agreement," or a promo that "can change."
  Fine — just get the number and write it down.
